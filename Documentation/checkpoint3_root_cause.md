In this checkpoint, the aggregated marketing metrics from Checkpoint 2 were segmented by channel, region, and time to investigate the underlying causes of differences in campaign performance.

First, ROAS was analyzed by channel and region to determine whether the low efficiency of Paid Search and Paid Social was driven by specific geographical areas. The results showed that both paid channels maintained relatively low ROAS across all regions, indicating that the performance issue was not concentrated in a particular region.

ROAS was then analyzed by channel and month to examine whether the performance gap was limited to specific periods. Although Paid Search and Paid Social showed noticeable ROAS improvements in November, their ROAS remained substantially lower than Email and Organic Search throughout the analyzed period. The November increase may also be influenced by seasonal factors and should therefore be interpreted carefully.

Conversion Rate was analyzed by channel and month to evaluate whether differences in conversion effectiveness could explain the performance gap. Paid Search and Paid Social consistently showed lower Conversion Rates than Email and Organic Search, with Paid Social performing particularly poorly.

Conversion Rate was also segmented by region. The results showed that the lower conversion effectiveness of Paid Search and Paid Social was consistent across regions, further indicating that the issue was not region-specific.

Finally, CPA was calculated by channel and by channel-region combination to evaluate the cost of acquiring a conversion. Paid Search and Paid Social had substantially higher CPA than Email and Organic Search, and this pattern remained consistent across regions.

Overall, the analysis indicates that the performance gap is primarily channel-level rather than region-specific or limited to a particular time period. Paid Search and Paid Social are significantly less efficient, as reflected by their lower ROAS and Conversion Rates and their substantially higher CPA.
