Marketing Performance Analysis
Case

The objective of this analysis is to evaluate the effectiveness of marketing campaigns across different channels, regions, and time periods. The analysis focuses on overall marketing performance, channel efficiency, ROAS, conversion rate, and cost per acquisition (CPA) to identify the main drivers of performance and potential areas for improvement.

Findings

The analysis shows significant differences in performance across marketing channels.

Email and Organic Search are the most efficient channels overall, with substantially higher ROAS and lower CPA than the paid channels, while also maintaining stronger conversion performance. In contrast, Paid Search and Paid Social demonstrate significantly lower ROAS and higher acquisition costs.

The channel-level performance gap is consistent across East, North, South, and West regions. This indicates that the lower performance of Paid Search and Paid Social is primarily related to the channels themselves rather than being caused by a specific region.

The analysis over time also shows that ROAS and conversion rates generally improve in November, particularly across the stronger-performing channels. This may indicate a seasonal effect that should be considered when evaluating campaign performance and planning future budgets.

Recommendations

Marketing budget allocation should be reviewed based on channel efficiency. A greater share of the budget could be allocated to Email and Organic Search, which demonstrate stronger returns and lower acquisition costs.

Paid Search and Paid Social should be further optimized rather than evaluated solely by traffic volume. Campaign targeting, audience segmentation, bidding strategies, creative performance, and landing-page effectiveness should be reviewed to identify the causes of their lower efficiency.

The November performance increase should also be investigated further to determine whether it is driven by seasonal demand, promotional activity, or other campaign-specific factors. If the pattern is confirmed across multiple periods, seasonal adjustments should be incorporated into future campaign planning and budget allocation.

Overall, the analysis suggests that marketing performance is primarily driven by channel efficiency, while regional differences have a relatively limited impact on the observed performance gap.
